Selective and Calibrated Decoding for Quantum Error Correction

A reproducible benchmark that evaluates a complete decoder family on a single defect-graph interface across five structured-noise regimes, treating the logical error rate, the expected calibration error, and the risk–coverage trade-off as first-class objectives — accompanied by analytic 95% confidence intervals and a code-level audit gate. We document statistical equivalence on the raw logical error and localise the discriminating signal in selectivity and calibration.

Background & motivation

A stabilizer code protects information only when its decoder maps a measured syndrome to the correct recovery coset — with low latency, and under the noise that physical hardware actually exhibits. The majority of decoder studies optimise a single scalar, the logical error rate, almost invariably under independent-and-identically-distributed (depolarising or bit-flip) noise. We contend that two additional axes are at least as consequential for deployable decoding, yet are routinely relegated to secondary status.

Noise is structured

Hardware noise is biased (Pauli-dominated), spatially bursty, neighbour-correlated, and attended by faulty syndrome read-out. A decoder that excels under i.i.d. noise may forfeit its advantage under structure; the sole means of resolving this question is to evaluate the identical family across regimes on a common footing.

Decoders may abstain

A decoder operating within a fault-tolerant loop is under zero obligation to commit to every syndrome. It may instead flag a shot as unreliable, deferring to a slower fallback decoder, a repeated measurement, or a higher-level protocol. This constitutes the QEC analogue of selective prediction.

Abstention requires calibration

Abstention confers value only when the confidence that gates it is calibrated — when a decoder reports 0.8, it should be correct 80% of the time. Coverage and calibration are routinely omitted from decoder benchmarks.

The gap addressed. Prior work examines matching, belief propagation, and learned and reinforcement-learning decoders in isolation and under i.i.d. noise, optimising the logical error rate. Existing benchmarks stop short of situating a complete decoder family on a single defect-graph interface across structured-noise regimes while elevating selective decoding and calibration to primary, statistically accounted-for axes. This is precisely the gap the present work addresses — a measurement methodology rather than a new state-of-the-art error rate.

Mathematics & method

Decoding is formulated as a problem on the code's defect graph. Eight interchangeable decoders consume one syndrome and return a correction together with a confidence; the figure of merit is the exact logical-failure indicator.

Syndromes & the coset choice

For the distance-\(d\) repetition code, \(d\) data bits over \(\mathbb{F}_2\) carry \(d-1\) weight-two checks \(Z_iZ_{i+1}\). A syndrome bit fires precisely where an error chain traverses link \(i\):

$$s_i \;=\; e_i \oplus e_{i+1},\qquad i = 0,\dots,d-2.$$

Two errors yield identical syndromes if and only if they differ by the logical operator, whence decoding reduces to a minimum-weight coset selection, and a correction \(\hat c\) succeeds if and only if the residual is a stabilizer with zero logical value:

$$\hat c = \arg\min_{c\,:\,\sigma(c)=s}\ \mathrm{wt}(c),\qquad \text{logical error}\iff \mathrm{parity}\!\big(e\oplus\hat c\big)=1.$$

The decoder interface & family

Every decoder implements one interface, which renders the family directly comparable and permits abstention and calibration to be defined uniformly:

$$\texttt{decode(syndrome)}\ \longrightarrow\ (\text{correction}\in\mathbb{F}_2^{\,n},\ \text{confidence}\in[0,1]).$$

lookup (optimal minimum-weight coset leader) · majority (integrate the syndrome, select the lower-weight coset) · matching (MWPM via Edmonds' blossom; defect-fraction confidence \(\exp(-2\,n_{\mathrm{def}}/n_{\mathrm{checks}})\)) · BP (min-sum, deferring to matching) · neural (logistic-regression coset selector; confidence equals the predicted-class probability) · topology (matching recalibrated by defect geometry) · fused (matching and neural combined by confidence) · rl (contextual bandit routing by defect-count context).

Calibration (ECE)

A confidence score is informative for abstention only when it faithfully reflects its own accuracy. The ECE partitions shots into ten equal-width confidence bins and averages the discrepancy between mean confidence and mean accuracy, weighted by bin occupancy:

$$\mathrm{ECE} \;=\; \sum_{\text{bins}} \frac{n_{\mathrm{bin}}}{n}\, \Big|\,\overline{\mathrm{conf}}(\mathrm{bin}) - \overline{\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{bin})\,\Big|,\qquad \overline{\mathrm{acc}} = 1-\overline{F}.$$

\(\mathrm{ECE}=0\) signifies that the stated confidence coincides with the realised accuracy in every bin.

Selective risk–coverage

Fix a confidence threshold \(\tau\) and commit exclusively on shots with confidence \(\ge\tau\). Then coverage\((\tau)\) denotes the committed fraction and risk\((\tau)\) denotes the logical error rate on the committed shots. Sweeping \(\tau\) traces the risk–coverage curve.

Theorem (monotone selective risk under calibrated confidence). Let \(F\in\{0,1\}\) be the failure indicator and \(C\in[0,1]\) a confidence with \(\Pr(F=0\mid C=c)=c\) a.s. For \(\Pr(C\ge\tau)>0\), the committed risk satisfies $$R(\tau)=\Pr(F=1\mid C\ge\tau)=1-\mathbb{E}[C\mid C\ge\tau],$$ which is non-increasing in \(\tau\).

The theorem is not presupposed in the experiments; empirical confidences are imperfect, and the risk–coverage curves and ECE therefore quantify the departure of each decoder from this ideal.

Experimental design & statistics

All reported quantities derive from the reported-scale configuration: repetition code, distance \(d=9\), \(40{,}000\) Monte-Carlo trials per (regime, decoder), \(200{,}000\) pooled shots per decoder, \(20{,}000\) training shots, evaluation \(p=0.12\), an eleven-point physical-error grid, seed 0. The run completes end-to-end in \(\approx 507\,\mathrm{s}\) at \(\approx 200\,\mathrm{MB}\) peak memory on a single CPU.

Sample size & CIs

For each (regime, decoder) pair we record a per-shot Bernoulli failure indicator. The logical error rate is its mean; the analytic 95% CI half-width is

$$\mathrm{ci}_{95}=1.96\,\hat\sigma/\sqrt n,\quad \hat\sigma\approx\sqrt{\bar p(1-\bar p)}.$$

At \(n=200{,}000\), \(\mathrm{ci}_{95}\approx\pm0.0016\).

Comparison set

The complete eight-decoder family (lookup, majority, matching, BP, neural, topology, fused, RL) across five structured-noise regimes: i.i.d., biased (sublattice, bias 3), burst (Poisson runs), correlated (neighbour recruitment), and measurement-error (read-out flips at \(q=0.05\)).

Integrity checks

Automorphism invariance: the matching decoder's logical error rate must remain invariant under cyclic relabeling over shifts \(\{1,2,d/2\}\) (flag automorphism_invariant = true). Audit gate: every reported number must trace to summary.json; proscribed superiority phrasing is rejected in continuous integration.

Calibration metric: ECE over ten equal-width confidence bins (above). Risk–coverage: the threshold is swept from the minimum to the maximum observed confidence, reported in descending-coverage order with a programmatic monotonicity check; committed-error CI bands employ an effective sample size of coverage \(\times\) pooled shots.

Raw error: a statistical equivalence

Logical error rates span merely 0.1516–0.1540, with every \(\mathrm{ci}_{95}\approx\pm0.0016\). The best-to-worst spread (0.0024) falls below one full interval width (\(\approx0.0032\)), so the decoders are not separated at the 95% level. Even the optimal lookup leader (0.1524) is in statistical equivalence with the heuristic decoders. We document the statistical equivalence rather than a superior method.

Abstention confers reliability

As the abstention threshold is swept, the committed error of matching declines monotonically from 0.1529 at full coverage to 0.0758 at \(\approx0.55\) coverage (an approximately twofold reduction for committing to half the shots) and to 0.0099 at \(\approx0.25\) coverage (a greater than fifteenfold reduction). The fused decoder traces a comparable curve: 0.0499 at \(\approx0.46\) coverage and 0.0101 at \(\approx0.25\) coverage. Monotonicity is verified programmatically.

Calibration ≠ sophistication

The ECE spans an eight-fold range and is uncorrelated with complexity. Ordering (best→worst): neural 0.0317 < majority 0.0489 < lookup 0.0569 < fused 0.0608 < bp 0.1232 < rl 0.2387 < topology 0.2428 < matching 0.2608. The best-calibrated decoder is the lightweight neural selector; the worst is plain minimum-weight matching.

Structured noise separates regimes, not decoders

The per-regime pooled logical error rises by more than 100× with noise structure, whereas the decoders cluster within their (tight) CIs at each regime. The most difficult regime is syndrome read-out error.

A data-fidelity note

This ECE ordering directly contradicts a natural intuition — and an explicit claim in the project's own README — that the confidence-fused decoder is the best calibrated. It is not: at reported scale the fused decoder ranks fourth on ECE (0.0608), whereas the neural selector ranks first (0.0317). We resolve the discrepancy in favour of the regenerated artifact — every value reported here is the value written to summary.json. The conclusion that endures is structural: calibration must be measured, not inferred from accuracy or complexity. Integrity flag automorphism_invariant = true.

Conclusion & impact

A deployable QEC decoder must accomplish more than minimising an i.i.d. logical error rate: it must perform well under structured noise, recognise when to abstain, and report calibrated confidence. This benchmark measures all three for a complete decoder family on a single defect-graph interface, and reports — candidly — that the decoders are statistically indistinguishable on the raw logical error while differing sharply in calibration and in the reliability they confer through abstention.

What is established

Statistical equivalence on the raw error (a 0.0024 spread within a \(\approx\)0.0032 interval); abstention as a substantial, monotone mechanism (committed error reduced several-fold at a moderate coverage cost); and calibration decoupled from sophistication — the best-calibrated decoder is the simplest, not the most elaborate.

Transparent limitations

  • Toy codes exclusively (one-dimensional repetition; surface-like toy lattice, not a stabilizer-exact surface code).
  • Simulated structured noise rather than hardware syndromes.
  • Logical-error differences lie within the statistical noise; the distance-scaling (sub-threshold) study is deferred to future work.
  • Lightweight learned models (logistic regression, contextual bandit), a single distance.
  • A single author and single CPU environment; independent replication remains outstanding.

Outlook

Extending the framework to a full-scale surface code, to hardware syndromes, and to a distance-scaling study — while retaining selectivity and calibration as first-class metrics — constitutes the natural next step. The measurement methodology is the contribution, and it generalises beyond these toy codes.
