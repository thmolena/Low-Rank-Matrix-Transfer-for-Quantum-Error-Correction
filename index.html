The problem

A quantum code measures a syndrome — which parity checks fired — without disturbing the logical information. A decoder must turn that syndrome into a recovery that returns the system to the codespace without applying a hidden logical flip. The hard part is that real hardware noise is structured (biased, bursty, neighbour-correlated, with faulty read-out), and the same syndrome can come from many errors that differ by a logical operator. Picking the right coset is where decoders win or lose.

Why topology

Defects live on the code's Tanner / plaquette graph; their geometry (chains, clusters, gaps) is what tells matching and message-passing how to pair them.

Why abstention

A decoder that knows when it is unsure can decline to commit. Sweeping that threshold traces a risk-coverage curve — reliability you can dial.

Why RL

No single decoder wins on every syndrome. A contextual bandit routes each syndrome to the decoder most likely to succeed, learning the routing online.

Method

Eight interchangeable decoders read one syndrome on the code's defect graph; the reward is the exact logical-error indicator (is the residual \(e\oplus\hat c\) a stabilizer?).

Why the demo is exact

For the distance-\(d\) repetition code, the syndrome is the discrete derivative of the error chain, \(s_i = e_i \oplus e_{i+1}\). Two error patterns share a syndrome iff they differ by the logical operator (the all-ones chain), so decoding is exactly a coset choice:

$$\hat c \;=\; \arg\min_{c \,:\, \sigma(c)=s}\; \mathrm{wt}(c),\qquad \text{logical error} \iff \mathrm{parity}\big(e \oplus \hat c\big) = 1 .$$

The browser computes the syndrome, integrates it back to a chain, picks the lighter of the two cosets (matching / majority) or lets the fused rule flip the coset, and checks the residual parity exactly — the same operations the Python package runs, so the in-browser logical error rate is the trusted quantity.
