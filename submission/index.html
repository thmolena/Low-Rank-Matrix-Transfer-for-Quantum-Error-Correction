Transfer-Operator Decoding of Structured Noise in Quantum Error Correction

Molena Huynh

North Carolina State University, Raleigh, North Carolina 27695, USA

Manuscript prepared for submission to PRX Quantum (REVTeX 4.2). Eleventh study in a single-author program on spectral truncation of operators.

Code repository: github.com/thmolena/Topological-Graph-for-Decoding-in-QEC  ·  manuscript sources and the reproducibility package are in submission/ and submission/code/.

Abstract

Decoding a quantum error-correcting code is a problem of statistical inference: a syndrome is a noisy measurement, and choosing a correction is estimating the most probable logical class under the device's error distribution. This work introduces a channel-adaptive spectral decoder that makes this inference explicit—it represents the structured error channel by a truncated transfer operator (an inhomogeneous first-order Markov, i.e. classical matrix-product, probabilistic surrogate on the code's defect graph) estimated from a small calibration sample, and selects the maximum-a-posteriori coset. The dominant decoders—minimum-weight matching, belief propagation, and learned decoders—all approximate the minimum-weight coset leader, which is maximum-likelihood only for i.i.d. noise; this shared target makes eight otherwise dissimilar decoders statistically indistinguishable on a distance-d repetition code. The spectral decoder is Bayes-optimal for the structured channel, provably reduces to the leader under i.i.d. noise, and reports the exact model posterior as a confidence that is calibrated by construction. On a unified benchmark (distance 9; 100,000 pooled shots per decoder; five structured-noise regimes), it cuts the logical error of the i.i.d.-optimal leader by 44.0% under bias (z = 10.5), 33.0% under bursts (z = 16.6), and 21.8% under neighbour correlations (z = 11.6), driving its pooled logical error to 0.1291 against 0.1508–0.1542 for the eight structure-agnostic decoders. It ties the leader under i.i.d. (z = 2.0) and read-out (z = 0.3) noise, where no single-shot data-channel advantage exists, and its posterior is near-perfectly calibrated where the model matches the channel (expected calibration error 0.0007 under i.i.d. noise) yet overconfident on the corrupted read-out syndrome (0.324), the predicted model-misspecification failure. Every number traces to a single reproducible artifact. Channel-awareness, made tractable and provable, is the lever that separates decoders under realistic noise.

Contributions

  1. A statistical theory of decoding as channel-adaptive Bayesian inference. Theorem 1 identifies coset-MAP decoding as the Bayes rule of the induced logical-classification problem under 0–1 loss and exhibits its Bayes risk; Proposition 1 turns the structured-channel gap into an exact identity for the regret of the i.i.d.-optimal minimum-weight leader; Proposition 2 and Corollary 1 show that a single O(d) forward sweep of the transfer operator computes both coset probabilities exactly, so the plug-in decoder is channel-matched optimal with a confidence that is the coset posterior; Theorem 2 bounds the decoding error through the operator's spectral gap (an effective-distance statement); and Theorem 3 with Corollary 2 proves finite-sample concentration of the estimated operator and asymptotic Bayes optimality with vanishing calibration error. Complete proofs appear in the manuscript's theory section and proof appendix. The theory instantiates the program's premise that statistics is structural to quantum computing: the device speaks in Born-rule samples, and the decoder that wins is the one whose inference matches the sampling law.
  2. A channel-adaptive algorithm, released as an installable, audited artifact. The spectral decoder estimates the truncated transfer operator—an interpretable Bayesian surrogate for the noise channel, whose forward pass is the inference—from a small calibration sample (12,000 shots, the same data the learned baseline trains on) and decodes with the exact model posterior as its confidence. The implementation, the eight baselines, the five noise samplers, all metrics, and the audit gate constitute the pip-installable package specops-transfer-decoder, whose console command transfer-decoder-reproduce regenerates every figure, table, and reported number from fixed seeds.
  3. Regime-resolved empirical findings with audited uncertainty. At distance 9 with 100,000 pooled shots per decoder, the spectral decoder cuts the leader's logical error by 44.0% / 33.0% / 21.8% under biased / burst / correlated noise (z = 10.5 / 16.6 / 11.6) and pools to 0.1291 against the structure-agnostic band 0.1508–0.1542, while tying honestly where the theory forbids an advantage (i.i.d. z = 2.0; read-out z = 0.3). Its posterior confidence is near-perfectly calibrated where the model matches the channel (ECE 0.0007 under i.i.d. noise) and overconfident exactly where the theory predicts misspecification (read-out, 0.324). Abstention reduces matching's committed logical error fourteenfold (0.1525 → 0.0108 at 0.25 coverage). Every number is transcribed verbatim from the run's single source-of-truth artifact (results/summary.json) and gated by a code-level audit; the claim is scoped to simulated structured noise on toy codes.

Reproduce every result

All figures, tables, and in-text numbers in the manuscript are regenerated end-to-end, from fixed seeds, by one command of the reproducibility package: 

pip install specops-transfer-decoder
transfer-decoder-reproduce            # reported scale: ~5 min, ~206 MB, single CPU
transfer-decoder-reproduce --quick    # same pipeline at reduced scale: ~30 s

The run writes the single source-of-truth artifact results/summary.json, then rebuilds every figure and table body from it. An audit gate (scripts/audit_claims.py) verifies that every reported number traces to that artifact and that the central spectral-versus-leader claim is statistically evidenced. The quick mode is documented and deliberately does not overwrite the manuscript's reported-scale artifacts.
